\begin{abstract}
Mainstream mathematics relies on an illegal truncation error. By breaking the Taylor series at the second derivative, it invents smooth continuities where only quantized, disjoint boundaries exist. This paper uses the \textbf{Theory of Nearly Everything (T.O.N.E.)} framework to expose the identical structural collapse across the Horse Fallacy, the Barber Paradox, ZFC Set Theory, and the arithmetic of $1+1=2$. 
\end{abstract}

\section{The Empty Intersection Fallacy}
The foundational failure of formal mathematics is the assumption that properties can propagate across independent domains without an overlapping mediator. In horse induction ($k \to k+1$), the step from $1$ to $2$ fails because two isolated singletons $\{h_1\}$ and $\{h_2\}$ share an empty intersection ($\emptyset$). There is no middle bridge, causing the information loop to crash instantly.

\section{The Delusion of $1+1=2$ and the $-1/12$ Penalty}
To write $1+1$, one must define a closed Unit. Pretending that a second unit can be introduced inside that same identity without a transition boundary is utopian nonsense. When standard math forces this broken counting method to infinity ($1+2+3+\dots$), the system overloads from the missing third time derivative---the \textbf{Jerk} ($j = \dddot{x}$). The resulting output of $-1/12$ is not a cosmic truth; it is a mathematical ghost value, a raw penalty for using an amputated axiomatic framework.
